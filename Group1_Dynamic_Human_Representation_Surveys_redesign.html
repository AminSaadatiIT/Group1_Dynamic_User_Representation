Summary

Treating time as an optional extra feature is the mistake this survey is organised around correcting. Alabduljabbar and colleagues review every known way of folding time into a recommender, then do something you rarely see in a survey paper and count the literature itself: where it gets published, by whom, how often it is cited. The part I keep coming back to is the split between short-term and long-term interest. You cannot claim a user has changed their mind until you have said which of the two you are measuring and over what window. Get that wrong and what looks like drift is just somebody browsing on a Tuesday afternoon. Which is exactly the kind of thing that quietly ruins an evaluation.

Summary

Here is the observation the paper is built on. Someone opens a music app at night to unwind, then opens the same app two hours later to drown out a noisy office. Nothing about the person changed. The intent did. Jannach and Zanker argue this happens inside a single session, not only between sessions, which means a recommender that knows nothing but your history has no way to tell those two visits apart. They sort the existing work into three buckets (diversification, direct intent prediction, latent intent modeling) and then spend a good part of the paper on the fact that we do not really know how well any of it works. Testing in this area is weak. Their conclusion is that more signals and more context are needed, because no single click tells you much about why someone is there at all.

مشاهده‌ای که این مقاله رویش ساخته شده این است. کسی شب اپ موسیقی را برای آرامش باز می‌کند و دو ساعت بعد همان اپ را برای پوشاندن صدای شلوغ محل کار. چیزی در خودِ آدم عوض نشده؛ نیت عوض شده. جاناخ و زانکر استدلال می‌کنند این اتفاق درون یک نشست هم می‌افتد، نه فقط بین نشست‌ها، پس سیستمی که فقط تاریخچهٔ کاربر را می‌شناسد هیچ راهی برای تشخیص این دو بازدید ندارد. آن‌ها کار موجود را در سه دسته می‌چینند (تنوع‌بخشی، پیش‌بینی مستقیم نیت، مدل‌سازی نیت پنهان) و بعد بخش قابل‌توجهی از مقاله را صرف این می‌کنند که ما اصلاً نمی‌دانیم این روش‌ها چقدر خوب کار می‌کنند. آزمون در این حوزه ضعیف است. نتیجه‌گیری‌شان این است که به سیگنال و کانتکست بیشتری نیاز داریم، چون هیچ کلیکی به‌تنهایی زیاد دربارهٔ دلیل حضور کاربر نمی‌گوید.

Summary

A recommender that serves a worker and a manager at the same time has a problem, because the two want different things. The learner wants the course that unlocks the next role. The manager wants the cheapest course that closes a compliance gap. This review takes that clash seriously and builds a categorization of learning goals, so the two sides can be compared instead of argued about. What makes it useful is the split between what a person wants for themselves and what an organisation requires, plus a third layer for the limits sitting around both: time, budget, prior qualifications. Most of the field folds all of this into one blended score. The paper argues, fairly convincingly, that you cannot optimise a blended score, since the person and the organisation are not optimising the same thing. Worth reading if your system answers to more than one master.

Summary

Fairness in recommendation is a constraints problem wearing a social science coat. Something has to give: exposure across providers, parity between groups, or accuracy for the individual user. The authors organise the whole field around that trade-off, and the section worth your time is the one on constraint-based approaches, where fairness is written into the optimisation problem instead of patched onto the output afterwards. They also spend real effort separating what fairness means for the person reading the list from what it means for the providers who get buried by it, and they are not charitable about how often the second group got forgotten. Fairness-aware re-ranking is standard now. Deciding which constraint goes first is not.

Summary

Everyone agrees context matters. Nobody has defined it. That is the gap this review goes after, and it is an odd state of affairs for a field that has been studying context-aware recommendation for years. Mateos and Bellogín work through the literature and come back with three things: a framework that makes context representation explicit, a classification of the methods used to fold context in, and an assessment of how any of this is actually evaluated. Here context means time, location, and social situation. This is the paper to cite when you need to write down, formally and defensibly, how a system should represent the conditions a user is acting under.

Summary

This research article proposes RUNSRec, a review-enhanced universal sequence representation learning approach for transferable and personalized recommender systems. The authors address two major limitations of existing sequential recommendation methods: their dependence on explicit item IDs, which makes transferring models to new domains and handling cold-start items difficult, and the challenge of preserving individual user preferences while learning general behavioral patterns. To overcome these limitations, RUNSRec combines item identity text with informative keywords extracted from user reviews to learn more meaningful item representations. It then uses a Transformer-based sequential model with contrastive learning to learn universal behavioral patterns across different domains. Finally, a lightweight personalized adapter uses user review information to capture individual preferences while keeping most of the pretrained model unchanged. Experiments on multiple Amazon datasets show that RUNSRec performs competitively or better than existing methods, particularly for long-tail items and users with few interactions. Overall, the paper demonstrates how textual information and user reviews can be used to learn transferable representations while preserving personalized user preferences.

ترجمه و خلاصهٔ فارسی

این مقاله یک روش به نام RUNSRec برای Universal Sequence Representation Learning (یادگیری نمایش ترتیبی عمومی) در سیستم‌های توصیه‌گر ارائه می‌دهد. نویسندگان به دو مشکل اصلی روش‌های توصیه‌گری ترتیبی می‌پردازند: وابستگی به شناسه آیتم‌ها که انتقال مدل به حوزه‌های جدید و مدیریت آیتم‌های جدید با سابقه کم را دشوار می‌کند، و مشکل حفظ ترجیحات شخصی کاربران در کنار یادگیری الگوهای عمومی رفتاری. RUNSRec برای حل این مشکلات، اطلاعات متنی آیتم‌ها و کلمات مهم استخراج‌شده از نظرات کاربران را ترکیب می‌کند تا نمایش بهتری از آیتم‌ها ایجاد کند. سپس با استفاده از یک مدل ترتیبی مبتنی بر ترانسفورماتور و یادگیری کنتراستی، الگوهای رفتاری عمومی را در حوزه‌های مختلف یاد می‌گیرد. در مرحله آخر، یک ماژول شخصی‌سازی از اطلاعات نظرات کاربران برای ثبت ترجیحات فردی آن‌ها استفاده می‌کند. آزمایش‌ها روی چند مجموعه‌داده Amazon نشان می‌دهند که RUNSRec در بسیاری از موارد عملکردی بهتر یا قابل رقابت با روش‌های موجود دارد، به‌خصوص برای آیتم‌های کم‌طرفدار و کاربرانی که تعاملات کمی دارند. در مجموع، مقاله نشان می‌دهد که می‌توان از اطلاعات متنی و نظرات کاربران برای ساخت نمایش‌های قابل انتقال بین حوزه‌ها استفاده کرد و هم‌زمان ترجیحات شخصی کاربران را حفظ کرد.

Summary

This survey provides a comprehensive review of the use of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) in recommender systems and analyzes more than 100 existing studies. The authors explain that recommendation data, such as user-item interactions, item sequences, social relationships, and knowledge graphs, naturally form graph structures. GNNs can use these structures to learn rich user and item representations by propagating information between connected nodes. The survey organizes existing approaches into several categories, including collaborative filtering, sequential recommendation, social recommendation, and knowledge-graph-based recommendation, and compares different methods for graph construction, neighbor aggregation, information propagation, and representation learning. It also discusses commonly used datasets, evaluation metrics, and real-world applications. Finally, the authors identify important challenges and future directions, including scalability, dynamic graphs, self-supervised learning, robustness, privacy, fairness, and explainability.

Summary

This paper investigates how user profiles can be modeled and dynamically updated in intelligent and personalized systems. It addresses the limitation of static user profiles, which may become outdated as users' interests, preferences, behaviors, and contextual needs change over time. The authors conduct a narrative review of the literature published in this area, searching Web of Science, ACM Digital Library, and Google Scholar and analyzing 117 publications. The reviewed studies are organized around user profile modeling, profile dynamics, recommender systems, personalized systems, and adaptive systems. The review examines how explicit and implicit user information can be collected and represented through approaches such as keyword-based profiles, semantic representations, embeddings, matrix factorization, and knowledge graphs. It particularly highlights the importance of modeling both short-term and long-term user interests and continuously adapting profiles to behavioral and contextual changes. The findings indicate that combining different types of user information and incorporating context-awareness and profile updating mechanisms can improve personalization and system adaptation. However, several challenges remain, including integrating heterogeneous and multimodal user data, enabling scalable real-time profile updates, preserving user privacy, improving contextual adaptation, and developing more comprehensive user representations. The paper concludes that dynamic user profiling is essential for maintaining accurate and effective personalization as users evolve over time.

Summary

This paper addresses a limitation in continual user representation learning: existing approaches are commonly evaluated without considering the passage of time between tasks, even though new items continuously emerge and change the item distribution in real-world recommender systems. The authors introduce a practical time-aware evaluation scenario and propose DITTO (Dynamic Time-aware Continual User Representation Learner), a continual learning framework designed to maintain a universal user representation while reducing catastrophic forgetting under continuous distribution shifts. DITTO uses two complementary knowledge-transfer mechanisms: a distribution-aware Forward Knowledge Transfer (FKT) module, which selects users with behavior distributions suitable for generating reliable pseudo-labels and transferring knowledge from previous tasks, and a distribution-aware Backward Knowledge Transfer (BKT) module, which uses users affected by distribution shifts to adapt previously acquired knowledge to the current distribution. The authors evaluate DITTO on real-world datasets under sequential tasks with different timestamps and compare it with state-of-the-art continual and transfer learning methods. The experiments show that DITTO consistently outperforms the baselines in the practical time-aware scenario, remains robust to different task orders and noisy tasks, and works with different backbone networks. The study demonstrates that dynamic user representation learning must account for temporal changes and evolving item distributions rather than relying on static task settings.

ترجمه و خلاصهٔ فارسی

این مقاله به یکی از محدودیت‌های یادگیری مداوم نمایش کاربر می‌پردازد: روش‌های موجود معمولاً بدون در نظر گرفتن گذر زمان میان وظایف ارزیابی می‌شوند، درحالی‌که در سیستم‌های توصیه‌گر واقعی، آیتم‌های جدید به‌طور مداوم ایجاد می‌شوند و توزیع آیتم‌ها را تغییر می‌دهند. نویسندگان یک سناریوی ارزیابی عملی و مبتنی بر زمان معرفی کرده و چارچوب DITTO (Dynamic Time-aware Continual User Representation Learner) را پیشنهاد می‌کنند که هدف آن حفظ یک نمایش عمومی از کاربر و کاهش فراموشی فاجعه‌بار در شرایط تغییر مداوم توزیع داده‌هاست. DITTO از دو سازوکار مکمل برای انتقال دانش استفاده می‌کند: ماژول Forward Knowledge Transfer (FKT) که کاربرانی با توزیع رفتاری مناسب را برای تولید برچسب‌های شبه و انتقال دانش از وظایف قبلی انتخاب می‌کند، و ماژول Backward Knowledge Transfer (BKT) که با استفاده از کاربرانی که بیشترین تغییر در توزیع رفتارشان را داشته‌اند، دانش قبلی را با توزیع فعلی سازگار می‌کند. آزمایش‌ها نشان می‌دهند DITTO در سناریوی مبتنی بر زمان عملکرد بهتری از روش‌های مقایسه‌ای دارد و در برابر تغییر ترتیب وظایف و داده‌های نویزی مقاوم است. این مطالعه نشان می‌دهد که یادگیری پویای نمایش کاربر باید تغییرات زمانی و تحول توزیع آیتم‌ها را در نظر بگیرد و نمی‌تواند صرفاً به تنظیمات ایستای وظایف متکی باشد.
